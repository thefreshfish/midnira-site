Penetration tests that cover more ground, led by an operator who has done it by hand.

Every penetration test is limited by hours. In an agentic test, AI agents take on the wide, repetitive work of mapping the attack surface, trying the known paths and re-checking fixes. That leaves the operator's time for the chained, context-dependent attacks that tools miss. Every finding in your report has been reproduced by a person.

What we test

Web applications and APIs
Authentication, authorization, business logic and the paths between them.
Networks
External exposure and internal movement, from the first foothold to what an attacker could reach.
Cloud and identity
Misconfigurations, excess permissions and trust relationships across cloud accounts and identity providers.

Rules of engagement

The terms every test runs under

  1. Written authorization comes first. No testing starts without a signed scope, a test window and emergency contacts.

  2. Agents stay in scope. Targets are checked against the authorization before an agent touches them, and every action is logged.

  3. A person validates every finding. Nothing goes into your report on an agent's word alone.

  4. Nothing disruptive without consent. Techniques that could affect availability or data need your approval in advance.

  5. AI use is disclosed. Your agreement names the AI tooling involved and explains how your data is handled.

A sample finding, as it appears in a report

Invoice API returns other customers' records

Severity
High
Where
GET /api/invoices/{id}

What happened

Changing the invoice number in a request returned an invoice that belongs to a different customer. The server checks that the caller is signed in. It does not check that the invoice is theirs.

Steps to reproduce

  1. Sign in as test customer A and open any invoice.
  2. Note the invoice number in the request, then request the next number up.
  3. The response contains customer B's name, address and line items.

Fix

Check ownership on the server for every invoice request, and add a test that fails if one customer can read another's invoice.
